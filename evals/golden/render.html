01 · Profile

Neerav at a Glance

Neerav U., eighth grade at Sierramont Middle School, San Jose. What he does, and how he works.
The business he built himself
He runs a Pokemon-card business with customers, and it has a goal attached.

He buys and sells Pokemon cards outside school, about four to six hours a week, and it makes a few thousand dollars a year. The goal his mother describes is to find cards that can be kept and become valuable enough to pay for a semester of college tuition, $5,000 to $10,000. Before this he ran an in-game Roblox business selling to other kids. Asked what he loves, he answers running my business and debating ideas.

What else he does
He competes across several areas, and tends to place when he does.

He has won medals in chess tournaments. He was a top-five finalist with a medal at a district-level cooking competition. He has played ping-pong for one to two years, one to three hours a week, and would like to try tennis instead. He performed in two elementary-school musicals. His interests run wider: writing and journalism, music, debate, business, languages and culture, history and politics, philosophy. He travelled to India, including the Ladakh mountains, river rafting and zip-lining, and wrote about that trip, and to France and on an African safari.

How he works best
He makes friends easily and people seek him out, which is where being able to lead a group starts.

He gets along with people and works well in a group. His mother names Leader among the words that describe him. He learns by listening and discussion. He thinks by debating: he takes apart something in politics or business, asks what if we did this versus that, and compares the outcomes. He works best when the concept is taught before it is tested, when instructions are clear, when there are milestones, and when a mistake has a route back, such as a retake.

What is in his favour, and what shapes the choices
A university-educated household, a first-generation American background, and money he already earns himself.

His mother holds a Columbia University degree and his stepfather is a scientist at Stanford — an academic household, though neither is a tie to any of the six schools on his list. He is a first-generation American from an immigrant family; because his mother holds a college degree, that is background rather than a first-in-family-to-college advantage. He lives in San Jose, inside the University of California's home state, and in his own words he is open to all cultures and foods. Two things shape the choices: a focused set of commitments he can sustain well, and in-person activity close to home, with autumn dates preferred because spring brings a long allergy season his mother describes as debilitating.

Flags to confirm

A few things to confirm with Neerav: the six colleges listed here, and roughly how many hours a week chess, cooking and theatre take. It would also help to know which organisation ran the chess tournaments and the district cooking competition, since that decides what those results can be entered into later.

A final note

You wrote that he has a real business and real potential, and that what you do not want is that energy scattered. This plan is built to the same view. It adds nothing he did not already choose: the business stays exactly as it is, debate is where his own words already point, and the volunteering is the thing you asked for. Everything else he does is protected and asked nothing of.

The first steps are deliberately small and finishable. A one-day fair in October has a clear beginning and end. Sampling debate formats commits him to nothing. Where a result is involved, it comes through a coached team with many rounds and practice in between, so one round never decides anything on its own. The number of activities he carries is capped at two or three, and that choice is made in tenth grade, with a year and a half of trying things first. He is good at this when he has picked it himself and someone is behind him; the plan is arranged so both stay true.
