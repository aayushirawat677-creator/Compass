# Compass Expert Corpus — College Admissions Playbook (v1)

> **What this is:** A structured knowledge base distilled from Peggy's expert source material (Drive → Peggy → Database). It acts as the "expert brain" that Compass agents consult when diagnosing a student's profile, setting strategy, and planning actions grade by grade.
>
> **Built from:** 15 documents (10 Instagram transcripts, 5 webinar docs; 1 exact duplicate removed). Last updated: 2026-09-23.

---

## 0. How agents should use this corpus

1. **Retrieve by section ID** (e.g., `EC-03`, `ACAD-07`). Every rule has an ID so agent outputs can cite the rule they applied.
2. **Respect confidence tags.**
   - `[CONSENSUS]` — Stated by 2+ independent sources. Treat as default truth.
   - `[SINGLE]` — One source only. Use, but hedge.
   - `[CONTESTED]` — Sources disagree. See §15 and reason about the student's context before choosing a side.
   - `[DATA]` — A numeric claim. Check the source caveat; some figures are vendor marketing or time-sensitive.
3. **Context over rules.** Almost every principle here is relative to the student's *school context* (school profile, courses offered, peer group). Never apply a rule without first asking "relative to what?"
4. **Target tier matters.** Most advice targets **Ivy+ / highly selective** schools (≤10% admit rate). For students targeting less selective schools, relax intensity-related rules (rigor maxing, national awards) but keep authenticity, depth, and narrative rules.
5. **Never recommend** pay-to-play research, bought passion projects, fabricated claims, or AI-written essays (see §14 Red Lines).

## 1. Core philosophy (the 12 first principles)

These show up in every source. Every agent recommendation should be traceable to at least one of them.

| ID | Principle | Tag | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P-01** | **Depth over breadth.** AOs want students who "went further than their peers in one or more areas," not the well-rounded student. "Well-rounded" is a myth. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-SUM, CRS-ACA |
| **P-02** | **Be a "one of one," not "one of 10,000."** The typical profile (all A's + piano + a sport + some competitions) blends in. Distinctiveness is what makes a student hard to reject. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-SUM |
| **P-03** | **Academics are the baseline, not the differentiator.** Without strong grades and rigor, nothing else matters at Ivy+. Schools sell education and don't want students who'll struggle. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-ACA, CRS-SUM |
| **P-04** | **Everything is judged in context.** You're compared to your school's offerings and peers (via the school profile), not to students everywhere. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-ACA, CRS-SUM |
| **P-05** | **Impact beats titles.** Stacking leadership titles means nothing. AOs look for evidence of responsibility, organizing others, and measurable impact. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-ACA |
| **P-06** | **Authenticity is non-negotiable.** AOs "see right through" manufactured, bought, or parent-driven activities. "Perfection is the enemy of progress. Authenticity always." | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-SUM |
| **P-07** | **Strategy before execution.** Build a narrative strategy *before* writing essays or filling out the activity list. Without it you're "throwing unconnected information" at a reader with no time to connect it. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-ACA, CRS-SUM |
| **P-08** | **The hook / unifying narrative.** Academics, ECs, honors, essays, and recs should tell one coherent story an AO can champion at committee. | [CONSENSUS] | CRS-ACA, CRS-SUM, ADM ("narrative") |
| **P-09** | **Time is the scarcest resource.** Cut "zombie activities." Do fewer things intentionally and well. Hope is not a strategy. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM |
| **P-10** | **Show how you'll use the school, not how great it is.** Top schools know they're great. They want to know how you'll take advantage of their resources and contribute to the community. | [CONSENSUS] | CRS-SUM, CRS-ACA |
| **P-11** | **Courage to step outside school.** If all ECs happen inside school (DECA, debate, MUN, robotics), you're not distinguishing yourself. Take interests into the real world. | [SINGLE → strong] | ADM |
| **P-12** | **Start early, but middle school is for building skills, not collecting prestige.** Profiles take years. Middle school achievements never appear on the application. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-SUM, CRS-ACA |

## 2. Academics

### 2.1 Rigor

| ID | Rule | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACAD-01** | Take the most rigorous curriculum available *relative to your school*. AOs read the transcript line by line alongside the school profile. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-ACA, CRS-SUM |
| **ACAD-02** | The right answer to "easy classes with A's vs. hard classes with B's" is **"C: hard classes with A's."** A 4.0 in easier classes ranks below A's in harder ones. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM |
| **ACAD-03** | Choosing courses based on what feels safe is noticed, especially during grade inflation. Consistently avoiding honors/AP stands out "but not in a good way." | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ACAD-04** | You don't need AP in *every* subject. Lean in fully on your intended field. Some top schools say an English major doesn't need AP Calc BC. Heavy EC involvement can balance a slightly lighter load. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ACAD-05** | Even humanities applicants are expected to show excellence across all core subjects, including math and science. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **ACAD-06** | **Standard Ivy+ prep:** 4 yrs English, 4 yrs math, lab sciences (bio, chem, physics), 3–4 yrs history, 4 yrs of *one* foreign language (don't switch). Google "[school] high school preparation" for each college's stated expectations. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **ACAD-07** | **Strong STEM progression:** math up to AP Calc BC, or Multivariable Calc / Linear Algebra if offered. Science up to AP Physics (C Mech / E&M) or AP Chem by senior year. Don't skip physics. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **ACAD-08** | **Strong humanities progression:** AP Lang (11th) → AP Lit (12th). Social science up to AP Gov / Macro / Micro, plus APUSH / AP World. Language to AP level. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **ACAD-09** | Business-interested students should be on track for **AP Calc BC by junior year**. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ACAD-10** | **AP vs IB:** top colleges have no preference. Pick based on learning style. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **ACAD-11** | **Honors ≠ AP**, usually. AP exams give an apples-to-apples comparison across schools and countries. Aim for AP where offered. | [CONSENSUS] | CRS-ACA, ADM |
| **ACAD-12** | **Dual enrollment** is fine and not viewed as weaker, but it's not the same as an AP exam score. Check GPA and class-rank implications with the counselor first. | [CONSENSUS w/ nuance] | CRS-ACA, ADM |
| **ACAD-13** | **Electives matter** once core classes are strong or placed out. Some should relate to the intended field; the rest can be personal interest (e.g., forensics). | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ACAD-14** | If not on an honors track in 9th grade, **fix it in 10th** (summer courses, appeal to the school). Switching gets very hard by junior year. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ACAD-15** | Junior-year grades, especially **second-semester junior year**, are the last transcript ED/EA readers see. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **ACAD-16** | B's and C's in core classes freshman year effectively rule out Ivy+. "The idea that you can bomb freshman year is old school." | [SINGLE] | ADM |

| ID | Rule | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

## 3. Extracurriculars

### 3.1 Portfolio structure

| ID | Rule | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EC-01** | "I'd rather have **4 outstanding activities than 10 average ones**." | [CONSENSUS] | ADM |
| **EC-02** | **Target shape:** at least 8 activities "in your orbit." Only 2–4 get deep investment (leadership, impact). The rest show other sides of personality. Filling all 10 Common App slots with filler hurts: 7–8 meaningful ones beat 10 with fluff. 15 activities is "literally too many." | [SINGLE → repeated] | ADM |
| **EC-03** | **Activity audit:** sophomore year (by sophomore summer at the latest), list every activity with days, hours per day, and hours per week. Rank by enjoyment. Drop "zombie activities" kept out of guilt. Put off, this decision leads to a scattered sophomore year and missed junior leadership. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **EC-04** | **Quit or stick?** Quit an activity you no longer enjoy, but first ask whether it's a bad moment or the activity no longer serves you. Replace it with something more meaningful. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **EC-05** | **Mix three EC types:** (a) *Institutional*: structured or competitive (Olympiads, ISEF, John Locke, Conrad Challenge, selective summer programs, school clubs). (b) *Innovative*: self-directed creation (venture, nonprofit, creative work such as a novel, poetry book, film, or podcast). (c) *Independent*: intellectual depth (research, self-studied advanced coursework, portfolio). | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **EC-06** | **Contribute to your own high school.** It's your first community after family. Useful test: "Will you be missed after you graduate?" | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **EC-07** | **Take school interests into the real world.** Every engineering applicant does robotics, every journalist does the school paper, every future lawyer does mock trial. Reframes: robotics → mentor middle schoolers or design something for the community. Journalism → publish in a local paper or start a niche Substack. Law → volunteer at a legal aid clinic or shadow an attorney. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **EC-08** | **Common profiles can still work.** A typical engineering profile (robotics, AP Physics, Calc BC, Olympiads, coding club) is common but fine. *How* you do things matters more than *what* you do. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **EC-09** | **Add a surprising second dimension.** Recommend clubs unrelated to the stated interest (a CS student in improv, dance, or esports). "There's not going to be a lot of CS kids who are also president of the improv club." | [SINGLE] | CRS-SUM |
| **EC-10** | **Branching out is authentic.** Not everything needs to fit a neat major silo, but an AO should still be able to *logically deduce the intended major* from activities and honors. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **EC-11** | **Leadership = evidence of responsibility**, not a position. Stepping into an existing organization and leading there is often better than starting something new. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM |
| **EC-12** | **Build the activity list continuously.** Keep a running list (paper on the wall works) because students forget things from earlier years. Exclude middle school items, except to note duration if an activity started then ("doing this for 8 years"). | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **EC-13** | **Quantify scope.** Include metrics (e.g., "top 300 STEM students nationally," "top 150 USNCO") so the AO understands the competition and scale in a fast read. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |

### 3.3 Specific activity types

| ID | Activity | Guidance | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EC-20** | **Jobs (especially service or retail)** | Possibly one of the most important ECs. "It can't be faked." Signals grit, tenacity, teamwork, and responsibility (showing up, angry customers). Must be sustained: 1–2 hrs/week won't count. Contrast: a "job at your mom's friend's hedge fund" where "we all know you're not doing anything meaningful." Working to support family is highly valued. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-SUM |
| **EC-21** | **Summer job vs summer program** | ADM: "Summer job all the way." CRS: go for top programs, but take a job whenever possible too. A job can make a student more competitive for programs like Bank of America Student Leaders. | [CONTESTED — see §15] | ADM, CRS-SUM |
| **EC-22** | **Sports (non-recruited)** | Only continue if the student truly enjoys it. Not required. Credit is given for discipline and leadership, but non-recruited students who only do sports look one-dimensional ("What else do you bring?"). **Multiple sports seasons are "dangerous"** because they eat all free time. Consider moving from varsity to club. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-ACA |
| **EC-23** | **Sports (recruited track)** | Different rules. Heavy focus is justified. First question: "Be honest with yourself: are you on a recruited track?" | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **EC-24** | **Founding a nonprofit** | ADM: 0/10 for most students. It takes a "staggering amount of work," most end up "thin and performative," and it's better to join an existing nonprofit. | **EC-25** | **Passion projects** | ADM: 0/10 if pre-packaged or bought; junior year is "pretty much too late anyway"; AOs see through them. CRS-ACA: a "capstone" (= passion project) starting from "What problem needs solving? What community needs serving?", started early enough to scale. | [CONTESTED — see §15] | ADM, CRS-ACA |
| **EC-26** | **Capstone design** (CRS) | Should connect to the student's interests but doesn't have to match the intended major (unlike research, which should). Start early, then scale regional → state → national → international by junior year. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **EC-27** | **Family responsibilities** | 10/10. Caring for family members or watching siblings is fully valid and never looked down on. There's a dedicated Common App section for it. Put it in the activity list only if it's major and daily. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **EC-28** | **Pre-packaged research or internships** | "A sham." Admittedly refuses to sell them. AOs who read tens of thousands of files spot them immediately. | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS-SUM |

## 4. Research, original scholarship & third-party validation

| ID | Rule | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RES-01** | Top schools (Yale, Princeton, Harvard) value **evidence of original scholarship**: the ability to create new knowledge, not repeat existing knowledge. It's now expected of Ivy+ applicants in *any* major, including arts and humanities. | [CONSENSUS] | CRS-ACA, CRS-SUM |
| **RES-02** | **Why research matters more now:** COVID learning loss and unreliable grading systems ("smiley face grades") make grades less trustworthy. | [SINGLE] | CRS-SUM |
| **RES-03** | **Research must be real.** Real lab, real professor, real responsibility. **Pay-to-play research is worthless and AOs dislike it.** | [CONSENSUS] | ADM, CRS |

| **RES-05** | **Prefer community-driven over prestige-driven topics.** Example: a future econ/IB major studied how small businesses survive inflation and rising taxes, not how CEOs can earn more. It reads as a "worldly thinker who cares about community." | [SINGLE] | CRS-SUM |
| **RES-06** | **Getting research access:** elite universities rarely take high schoolers outside their own summer programs. **Email local or lower-ranked universities** instead (e.g., a University of Indianapolis ask instead of MIT). This has a high success rate. | [SINGLE] | CRS-SUM |
| **RES-07** | **Validate it externally:** publish in reputable journals, present at conferences, or both. Put links in the application so claims are easy to verify. | [SINGLE] | CRS-ACA |
| **RES-08** `[DATA]` | Caltech reportedly has a **"fraud squad"** that checks application claims. Never exaggerate. | [CONSENSUS] | CRS-ACA, ADM |

## 5. Summer programs

| ID | Rule | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SUM-01** | **Alignment, alignment, alignment.** The program must match the student's field and narrative. A Stanford math camp doesn't help a biology applicant. | [SINGLE → emphatic] | CRS-SUM |
| **SUM-02** | **Avoid pay-to-play and brand traps**, e.g., a "new AI program at a big private school" selling on buzzwords. Not all programs are equal; only a handful move the needle. | [CONSENSUS] | CRS-SUM, ADM |

| **SUM-08** | **Don't rely on one reach program.** With a ~2%-admit program as the only option, "you're not playing the game." Build a portfolio of applications. | [SINGLE] | CRS-SUM |

## 7. The hook (unifying narrative)

| ID | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **HOOK-01** | **Definition:** a unifying narrative connecting academics, courses, ECs, competitions, honors, essays, and recs into "a single, compelling, cohesive story." | CRS-ACA, CRS-SUM |
| **HOOK-02** | **Why it matters mechanically:** a regional AO reads first, then *presents and advocates* for the applicant at committee, where members vote. The hook gives the AO a pitch to remember and champion. | CRS-ACA |
| **HOOK-03** | **Distinguishing excellence:** exceptional depth in one or more areas that shows how the student will enrich the campus "mosaic." | CRS-ACA |
| **HOOK-04** | **Formula:** *[identity / discipline] + [what they did] + [impact on whom]*. Examples:<br>• "A scientist-humanist who investigated lead in their city's water and got local school fixtures tested and remediated."<br>• "A literary thinker who analyzed political philosophy and published essays connecting classical texts to modern social issues."<br>• "A civic-minded leader who studied public policy and mobilized peers to drive real change locally."<br>• "An ML engineer on a mission to improve the lives of people with Type 1 diabetes." (MIT / Yale admit)<br>• "A poet-activist editor-in-chief who scaled a literary nonprofit while becoming the state's inaugural youth poet laureate." (Princeton / Penn admit)<br>• "An origami artist-scientist fascinated by neurobiology, computational math, and rule-bound systems." (Yale REA admit) | CRS |
| **HOOK-05** | **What AOs root for (one ex-AO's view):** a "wholesome, do-good kid" who excels in specific areas and will improve the community, including service, community, and making the world better. | CRS-SUM |
| **HOOK-06** | **Quirky interests can become the hook** (origami). They grab attention, but the academic and EC framework underneath must be strong. | CRS-ACA |
| **HOOK-07** | **A clear narrative makes decisions easier.** Should they keep the long-running activity or do the academic program? Pick whatever fits the narrative. | CRS-SUM |
| **HOOK-08** | **Tailor the story to each application format.** UVA and Northeastern have no supplements; Yale, Harvard, and Stanford have heavy ones; the UC application differs from the Common App. Plan how the narrative shows up in each. | ADM |
| **HOOK-09** | **Changing direction is OK.** E.g., surgeon's child tries a medical internship and realizes it's not for them. Stop, adjust, *take decisive action*, and explain the shift in the application. | ADM |
| **HOOK-10** | **Genuine motivation beats parental career pressure.** If the student can't convincingly explain why they want the major (e.g., "mom and dad want me to be an investment banker"), it will show. | CRS-SUM |

## 8. Essays

### 8.1 Personal statement (Common App, 650 words)

| ID | Rule | Tag | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ESS-01** | **An admissions essay is not an English-class essay.** A beautifully written standalone memory ("my relationship with my mother," "buying a used car") that doesn't explain *who you are, how you got here, where you're going, and why it matters* is a **red flag → deny pile.** | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ESS-02** | The essay is the one place to show **personal characteristics that appear nowhere else.** You don't need your whole life history, just a story that's personal, true, and revealing. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ESS-03** | **Strategy first.** Decide what the AO needs to learn that the rest of the application doesn't show. Starting to write without a strategy means "you're toast." | [CONSENSUS] | ADM |
| **ESS-04** | **Don't read other students' essays.** What worked for them depended on their whole application. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ESS-05** | **Brainstorm process:** (1) Where am I going: what do I want to learn and accomplish in college? (2) What makes me unique and how will I contribute in and out of class? (3) What's my storytelling style? (4) Limit outside input; no outside editing. (5) Free-write until you find your voice, pause, reconsider the strategy, then write. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ESS-06** | **Write first, choose the prompt after.** Picking a prompt first pushes toward formulaic writing. Prompt 7 (topic of choice) is always available. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ESS-07** | **Worst advice heard:** (1) "You need a trauma story." (2) "Be creative." | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ESS-08** | **First-paragraph red flags:** no compelling hook, flowery language, complex grammar, tired cliché topics. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ESS-09** | **Never open with a quote or cliché.** Overused quotes make readers skip; obscure ones waste about 150 words on context. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ESS-10** | **Plain, relatable language.** AOs spend about 5 minutes per file and read fast. Big words and complex sentences annoy them. Powerful speakers use relatable words. | [SINGLE] | ADM |
| **ESS-11** | **Don't overclaim.** "You're 17. Scale it back, make it relatable and believable." Small, honest learning moments beat artificial grandeur. Include vulnerable, human details (crying on the bus) that create connection. | [SINGLE] | ADM |

## 10. College list, application strategy & institutional factors

| ID | Topic | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LIST-01** | **How many schools** | Applying to only 10 is risky today. Use a diversified portfolio of about 15 on the Common App, plus the UC app, plus standalone apps (e.g., MIT). The Common App caps at 20. **Don't apply anywhere the student wouldn't attend.** | ADM |
| **LIST-02** | **Visibility** | Colleges can't see a student's other schools. They *can* see when they're added under "My Colleges," email opens, and sometimes social media interactions. | ADM |
| **LIST-03** | **Demonstrated interest** | Check the Common Data Set. Top-25 schools usually say they don't consider it. It matters a lot at **small liberal arts colleges** that don't want to be treated as safeties (they use tracking software). Ways to show it: tours, virtual tours, webinars, newsletters, opening and clicking emails, social media, asking questions. Not visiting is never penalized; say so in Additional Info if relevant. | ADM |
| **LIST-04** | **ED/EA trend** | With the shift toward early decision, schools are trying to identify who will commit and pay full tuition, so demonstrated interest matters more at larger schools than before. | ADM |
| **LIST-05** | **Legacy** | Check the Common Data Set factor table. It's a **tiebreaker between equal applicants**, not a lift for weak ones. Definitions vary by school: at Harvard, only a parent who attended Harvard *College* counts (not grad school, grandparents, or cousins). Don't rely on it. | CRS-ACA, ADM |
| **LIST-06** | **Donations** | "No check you can write to Stanford" changes admission. A major-gift track historically required about a $3M gift and still didn't reach admissions' desk. | ADM |
| **LIST-07** | **Transfer myth** | Transferring into an Ivy is *not* easier. Some schools have single-digit transfer spots. Transfers need a real reason (why the current school isn't working, how the new one helps), not rank-jumping. | ADM |
| **LIST-08** | **Public vs private university** | A personal decision: weigh finances, opportunities, and long-term academic growth. | ADM |
| **LIST-09** | **Changing majors** | Students can change majors at almost every college. Applying under one major isn't a life sentence. | CRS-SUM |
| **LIST-10** `[DATA]` | **Admit rates (as cited)** | Ivy League average about 5% (CRS-SUM) *or* 9% (CRS-ACA). The sources conflict; ~5% is closer for the class of 2030. Stanford ~3.5% (class of 2030); Harvard ~3%; about 2018–2020, Ivy ~9–10% and Stanford ~5%. UCLA ~145,000 applicants; top schools get 40–60k. Seats are fixed while applications rise. | CRS, ADM |
| **LIST-11** | **Read time** | First read is **~5 min** (ADM) to **8–12 min** (CRS-ACA). Either way: make it effortless to understand. | ADM, CRS-ACA |

## 11. Grade-by-grade playbook

### Middle school (grades 6–8)

- **Purpose:** build skills, not prestige. Nothing from middle school goes on the application. [CONSENSUS: ADM, CRS-SUM]
- **Study habits:** classes get hard, so natural talent alone stops being enough. Build discipline, note-taking, and the habit of talking to teachers. [ADM]
- **Getting involved:** student government, sports, and activities build comfort with leadership. If school activities don't appeal, engage outside school. [ADM]
- **Social:** no need to be a social butterfly, but make friends across different types of people. [ADM]
- **Advanced classes: yes.** Getting on a fast track now opens advanced high school courses. [ADM]
- **Rising 8th grader:** focus on **leadership** (practice it, fail, recover) and **academic rigor**. [ADM]
- **What NOT to do:** no SAT prep and no internship planning for 7th graders. [ADM]
- **Parent role:** "Not to build a college application but to help build a young person." Create opportunities, ask questions instead of giving answers, and encourage curiosity and reflection. [ADM]
- **Consulting approach at this age:** a deep dive into interests (books, after-school activities, favorite classes and teachers) and the parents' goals. Coach and mentor, don't dictate. [ADM]
- Research-methods programs that teach hypothesis-driven thinking are fine as skill-building. [CRS-SUM]

### 9th grade — Discovery + foundations

- **Academics:** get the academic base right in the first term. Study methods, test prep, and teachers all change. "Freshman year does matter." [CONSENSUS: ADM, CRS]
- **Get onto honors / advanced tracks now** (see ACAD-14). [ADM]
- **ECs:** explore broadly. Join many clubs, try new things, find your people. Don't lock into a major identity yet. [CONSENSUS]
- **Competitions:** enter a few as experiments (HON-01). [CRS-SUM]
- **Summer after 9th:** short pre-college programs are acceptable, taking varied courses; or a job. [ADM, CRS-SUM]
- **Mistakes to avoid:** (1) following the herd and blending in; (2) not committing to academic excellence; (3) failing to plan ahead. [ADM]

### 10th grade — Narrow + decide

- **"Stop collecting activities and start making decisions."** Run the activity audit (EC-03). Cut to 2–3 main focuses by sophomore summer. [ADM, CRS]
- **Academics:** workload and intensity roughly double. Set up tutoring and study groups *before* you need them. Falling behind early in 10th is hard to recover from. [ADM]
- **Check trajectory:** Am I in the hardest classes available? On track for Calc BC by junior year (business/STEM)? Compare against classmates, since you'll be evaluated against them. [ADM]
- **Testing:** start an external-metric strategy (AP exams, SAT/ACT diagnostic). Check the middle-50% ranges in target schools' Common Data Sets. [ADM]
- **ECs:** local competitions, local internships, starting research, taking leadership steps in the chosen 2–3 activities. [CRS-ACA]
- **Summer after 10th:** a *substantive* program, research, or job, not a 1–2 week pre-college program. [ADM, CRS]

### 11th grade — Impact + peak

- **"What matters about junior year? Everything."** [ADM]
- **Academics:** heaviest rigor (e.g., case study: 7 APs). Second-semester grades are the last ED/EA readers see. [CRS-ACA]
- **Testing:** finish SAT/ACT; AP exams (aim for 5s). [ADM]
- **ECs:** take real leadership roles; **scale impact** (regional → state → national → international); enter national/international competitions. [CONSENSUS]
- **Recs:** build relationships with the 2–3 recommending teachers (REC-01). [ADM]
- **Passion projects started now are "pretty much too late"** (ADM). Scale existing work instead.
- **Time audit:** analyze every minute of the day critically, cut ruthlessly, but keep time for family and friends. [ADM]
- **Parents:** stay involved. "Hope is not a strategy." [ADM]

### Summer before 12th — Strategy + story

- **Develop the application strategy:** What have the past three years said about me? What needs to change? Where do I need more focus? [ADM]
- **Build the narrative and hook** before writing (P-07). [CONSENSUS]
- **Common App opens August 1.** Draft the personal statement early (possibly before October; see OXB-03). Fill in the forms early. [ADM, OXB]
- **Rising seniors without guidance:** ADM specifically advocates for them (content cut off in source).

### 12th grade — Curate

- **You can't build a profile in 2 months.** Senior fall is for *storytelling and curation*, not new impact. [CRS-SUM]
- Finalize the list (about 15 Common App schools + UC + standalone), essays, supplements, and the activity and honors lists ordered by strength with metrics. [ADM, CRS]

| 10 | MIT Beaver Works (glucose forecasting + insulin-control deep learning) | Debate (natural talent spotted); ran for and won president of a coding competition org | Grow the nonprofit into research. Pitched Beaver Works on the work, not the brand. |
| 11 | MIT PRIMES (~2%); Simons Research Fellowship | ISEF finalist; conference presentations | Go national. Research deepened out of genuine interest. |
| 12 | — | Regeneron finalist, state fair champion, USACO, International AI Olympiad bronze, congressional debate semifinalist | Curate the story. The strategist admits "he didn't need all these awards." |

**Lesson:** lived experience → service → research → national validation. Authentic and ROI-driven.

## 14. Red lines & decision rules for agents

### 14.1 Never recommend

1. Pay-to-play research or "pre-packaged" research internships (RES-03, EC-28)
2. Bought, off-the-shelf passion projects (EC-25)
3. Using AI to write, brainstorm, or proofread essays (ESS-12)
4. Exaggerating or fabricating claims (RES-08)
5. Using Additional Info as a second essay or overflow (ESS-31)
6. Applying to schools the student wouldn't attend (LIST-01)
7. Choosing a summer program for the brand alone or out of misalignment (SUM-01, SUM-02)
8. Pursuing a major only because of parental pressure (HOOK-10)
9. Donations as an admissions strategy (LIST-06)

### 14.2 If → Then heuristics

| If the student… | Then… | Rule refs |
|---|---|---|
| Has 10+ activities, all low-hours | Run an audit; cut to 2–4 deep plus 4 supporting | EC-01, EC-02, EC-03 |

| Plays 2–3 sports seasons, not recruited | Suggest dropping to one sport or club level to free time | EC-22 |
| Is a recruited athlete | Sport focus is justified; still add 1–2 non-athletic dimensions | EC-23 |
| Wants to start a nonprofit | Ask: will this be your main focus? If not, join an existing org and lead there | EC-24, EC-11 |

| Is not on an honors track in 9th/10th | Priority action: get onto the track (summer course, school appeal) | ACAD-14 |
| Has a 4.0 but a low-rigor schedule | Raise rigor next term; explain that rank reflects rigor | ACAD-02 |
| Attends a school with few APs | Self-study AP exams, dual enrollment; flag in school profile and Additional Info | ACAD-30, TEST-05 |
| Is homeschooled | AP exams, outside coursework, higher EC bar | ACAD-31 |
| Wants to do research | Find a community-relevant topic; email local universities; target a journal matching their level | RES-05, RES-06, §4.1 |
| Is picking summer programs | Filter by alignment, then selectivity and output; build a portfolio of applications; include a job option | SUM-01, SUM-05, SUM-08, EC-20 |

| Keeps an activity they no longer enjoy | Recommend quitting and replacing with something meaningful | EC-04 |

| Is targeting a small liberal arts college | Actively show demonstrated interest | LIST-03 |

### 14.3 Profile diagnostic checklist (for a GapAnalyst agent)

1. **Academic baseline:** rigor relative to school? GPA trend? AP/IB scores (5s)? Test scores vs target middle-50%?
2. **Spike:** is there 1+ area of distinguishing excellence? Can an AO name it in one sentence?
3. **Depth:** 2–4 activities with sustained multi-year investment and rising responsibility?
4. **Impact:** measurable outcomes (people served, money raised, publications, placements) with scale metrics?
5. **Validation:** third-party proof (publication, competition placement, selective program, press)?
6. **Real-world reach:** does anything extend beyond school walls?
7. **Authenticity:** does the student own it, or does it look bought or parent-driven?
8. **Coherence:** can you guess the intended major from the activities and honors?
9. **Dimension:** at least one surprising non-core interest?
10. **Character signals:** job, family responsibility, service, community orientation?
11. **Recs:** does the student have 2–3 teachers who know them well?
12. **Timing:** is the plan realistic for the student's current grade (see §11)?

## 16. Data caveats

- **Crimson statistics** (RSI → HYPS ~59–60%, "7.4× admit rate," "Kenyon → ~50–60% Harvard odds") come from vendor marketing webinars and internal data. They show **correlation, not causation**; RSI admits are already exceptional. The Kenyon claim is especially doubtful. Don't present these as facts to families without a caveat.
- **Admit rates** change every cycle. Refresh from official sources before using them.
- **Program details** (age limits, selectivity) change yearly. Verify before recommending.
- Program names were auto-transcribed in some places ("Promise" = PROMYS, "Curio" = Curieux, "Simmons" = Simons, "ISAF" = ISEF, "MITS" = MITES). Corrected here where confident.
